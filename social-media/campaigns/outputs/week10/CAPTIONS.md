## Day 3 — Final round (Theatre spotlight)
**Cover:** You made the final. Now own it.

### IG / FB
You made the final. They already like you.

The last round is rarely about proving you can do the job, you cleared that earlier. It's about fit: would they want you in the room every day? So show it. Reference the people you'd work with, back every claim with a number, ask one sharp question, and say clearly that you want the role.

Enthusiasm, stated out loud, breaks more ties than people think.

Swipe for the final-round playbook.

Find roles at internwise.co.uk

### LinkedIn
Reaching a final round means the hard part is done. The company already believes you can do the job. What they're deciding now is fit.

That shifts how you prepare. Learn who you'd actually work with and reference them. Re-tell your strongest story with the result they care about most, and have a number ready for every claim. Ask one question sharp enough to be remembered, such as "what does success look like in this role at six months?" Then close with intent: say plainly that you want the role and ask about next steps.

Enthusiasm, stated out loud, breaks ties more often than most candidates expect.

## Day 4 — After a rejection (Comic book / pop-art)
**Cover:** It's not the end. It's data.

### IG / FB
Got rejected? It's not the end. It's data.

Rejection is the default in a job hunt, not the exception, so the skill is what you do next. Feel it for 24 hours, then file it away. Ask for feedback in three short sentences. Turn that feedback into one concrete fix. Then reapply, because plenty of teams re-hire the candidate who improved and came back.

A "no" is round one, not the final whistle.

Swipe for the 4-step comeback.

Find roles at internwise.co.uk

### LinkedIn
A rejection feels personal, but the numbers say otherwise: only a small fraction of applicants get an offer from any single role. Rejection is the default. What separates people isn't avoiding it, it's how they respond.

A simple loop works. Let it sting for a day, then move on. Send a short, polite request for feedback and save every reply, because the patterns tell you what to fix. Turn that into one concrete change to your CV or your weakest interview answer. Then reapply, to that company or three like it, while your prep is fresh.

Candidates who ask for feedback and reapply give themselves far more chances than those who take one "no" as the final answer.

## Day 5 — Grad-scheme timeline (Airport departure board)
**Cover:** Grad schemes have a gate time.

### IG / FB
Grad schemes have a gate time. Miss the window and you wait a whole year.

Around 70% of schemes close before Christmas, and most run a single intake. So the calendar matters as much as the CV. Summer is for research and getting your CV and tests ready. September to December is peak departures, apply early. Miss the rush? January to spring is a real second wave of rolling intakes, not a dead end.

Your seat is there. You just have to board on time.

### LinkedIn
The biggest reason graduates miss out on schemes isn't ability, it's timing. Roughly 70% close applications before Christmas, and most open only one intake a year. Miss it and you wait twelve months.

Here's the timeline. Over summer, research schemes, build a target list, and get your CV and psychometric practice ready. From September to December the major schemes open and fill fast, so applying early genuinely beats applying late for the same role. If you miss the autumn rush, don't write off the year: January to spring brings rolling intakes, SME roles and next summer's internships.

Treat it like a departure board. The seat is yours if you're at the gate on time.
